# Citation Formats — Making Every Decision Locatable

A citation is only useful if the user can land on the exact moment. The locator depends on what the transcript provides. Detect the format first, then cite consistently throughout.

## Detect the format

Scan the transcript's structure before extracting:
- **Timestamps present?** Lines or turns prefixed with times like `00:42:15`, `[01:12]`, `1:23:45`, or `(14:05)`.
- **Speaker labels present?** Names or roles prefixing turns: `Sarah:`, `[John]`, `FACILITATOR:`.
- **Neither?** Plain prose or paragraphs.

Many transcripts have both timestamps and speakers — use both.

## Format A — Timestamped

Cite with the timestamp, the speaker if available, and a short exact quote.

> Reached: [00:41:30] Maria — "let's go with the phased rollout"
> Confirmed: [01:15:02] Maria — "so we're agreed, phased over three weeks"

If timestamps mark turn-starts rather than exact lines, the turn timestamp is fine — the user can read from there. Preserve the transcript's own timestamp style (don't convert `[01:12]` into `00:01:12` unless you're certain of the scale).

## Format B — Speaker-labeled, no timestamps

Cite with the speaker and a short exact quote. The quote is the locator — make it distinctive enough to find with a search.

> Reached: Maria — "let's go with the phased rollout"
> Reversed: David — "actually, scrap the phasing, we need it all live for the audit"
> Confirmed: Maria — "fine, big-bang on the 14th then"

Choose quote snippets that are unique in the transcript (avoid quoting a common phrase that appears many times). 4–12 words is usually enough.

## Format C — Plain text, no structure

Cite by quoting the exact passage, verbatim and short, in quotation marks. The quote *is* the locator since there's nothing else to point to.

> Reached: "the team agreed to go with the phased rollout approach"
> Confirmed: "to confirm, we'll phase the rollout over three weeks"

Keep quotes short but distinctive. If a decision spans a longer passage, quote the single most decision-bearing sentence rather than a long block.

## Format D — Mixed / inconsistent

Real transcripts are often messy — timestamps for some sections, not others; speaker labels that drop in and out. Use the richest locator available *at each point*. It's fine for one decision's trail to mix locator types if the transcript itself is inconsistent. Consistency of *findability* matters more than uniformity of format.

## Rules for all formats

- **Quotes are verbatim.** Exact transcript text inside quotation marks. Never paraphrase inside a citation — paraphrase defeats the purpose, which is verifiability.
- **Keep quotes short.** Enough to locate and confirm the point, not a transcript dump. One sentence or a distinctive phrase.
- **One locator per touch point.** Each stage of a decision's trail (reached, changed, confirmed) gets its own citation.
- **If you can't locate it, you can't cite it — and if you can't cite it, you can't claim it.** A decision without a findable citation is downgraded to "unclear / discussed but not clearly decided."
- **Don't fabricate locators.** Never invent a timestamp. If the transcript has no timestamps, use quotes — don't approximate a time.

## A note on speaker attribution

When citing who drove a decision, attribute only what the transcript clearly shows. If it's unclear who said something (overlapping speakers, unlabeled turn), cite the quote without forcing an attribution rather than guessing the speaker.
